## Start with Unanswered, not with a blank page

Don't try to write everything up front. Let a week of calls happen, then open **Knowledge Base**, then **Unanswered**. Every question there was asked by a real caller. Convert the useful ones to articles. Repeat weekly.

## One question, one answer

An article titled "Do you offer financing?" with a three-sentence answer will be found and read out cleanly. A document called "Everything about payments" will be searched, and the agent may read the wrong part.

## Write answers the way you'd say them

The agent reads the answer out loud, more or less as written. "Yes, we offer 12-month interest-free financing on jobs over $1,000 through Wisetack. The office can send you the application link." Short sentences, no bullet points, no links.

## Put the decision in the answer

If the honest answer is "it depends", say what it depends on and what the agent should do: "Prices depend on the size of the unit. Take the caller's name and number and someone will call back with a quote within one business day."

## Documents are for depth

Upload the price list, the FAQ page, the warranty terms. Let the agent search them for the details you'd never write articles for. Keep them current: remove and re-upload when they change.

## Check what the agent found

When an answer sounds wrong, open the call and look at **Unanswered questions** and the transcript. Then search your knowledge base for the same words the caller used. Often two articles overlap, or the caller's wording doesn't match yours. Add their wording to the question.

## Retire stale content

An old price list will be found and read. Delete documents you no longer stand behind.
