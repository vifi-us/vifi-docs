description: How to describe your business so the agent answers like someone who works there.

The agent reads your profile before every call. Written well, it answers most everyday questions on its own.

## Describe what you do in a caller's words

Callers ask "do you fix water heaters?", not "do you provide plumbing solutions?". Write the description with the things people actually call about:

> We're a family plumbing company serving the north side of Denver. We repair and replace water heaters, fix leaks and clogged drains, and install fixtures. We do emergency calls 24/7 with a $150 call-out fee after 6pm. We don't do new construction.

Notice what's in there: the area you serve, the top services, an after-hours policy, and what you don't do. Each one heads off a common question.

## Say what you don't do

"We don't service commercial buildings" saves a caller five minutes and saves you a wasted callback. The agent will say it politely.

## Set hours, even if they're complicated

Set the regular hours in the profile. Put the exceptions in the knowledge base: "We're closed on public holidays", "Saturday appointments are by request only". The agent combines both.

## Keep the services list short and specific

Ten clear items beat forty vague ones. "Furnace repair" and "Furnace installation" are better than "HVAC".

## Use the industry field

It changes the words the agent reaches for. A dental office and a body shop both "book appointments", but the agent will ask different questions on the way.

## Read it back as a caller

After saving, call your agent and ask "what do you do?" and "where are you?". If the answer sounds off, the description is the place to fix it.
